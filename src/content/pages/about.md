I grew up in Ludhiana, in a household where medicine was the family trade and computers were the thing I could not leave alone. In high school that curiosity turned into a habit of shipping: apps for patients and doctors, a book on quantum mechanics, papers I was stubborn enough to finish, and a student council job that lasted two years. I wanted to know whether an idea survived contact with other people — a clinic, a village, a reader — not only whether it compiled.

At Carnegie Mellon I am studying computer science, with minors in machine learning and mathematical finance. The through-line is still systems that have to work under constraint: live market data, hospital procedure logs, a university data catalog that other people depend on. I care about work that is technically honest and easy to inspect — the kind of artifact you can hand a teammate or a professor without a sales pitch.

I am trilingual in English, Hindi, and Punjabi. When I am not in a terminal I am usually on a tennis court, a soccer pitch, or a pickleball game, or stuck on a Sudoku.
